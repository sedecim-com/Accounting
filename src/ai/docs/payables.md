# Accounts payable (vendors and received invoices)

## Vendors
- Creating a vendor leaves an audit row (G3): who, when, and which fields — bank details never travel into `audit_log`, not even already-encrypted, because that table is append-only (033) and a CLABE that lands there does not come out even by rotating the key; what is recorded is `bank_details_on_file: true`.
- Data: vendor_number (V-...), company_name, tax_id (RFC/EIN), terms, default expense account, is_1099_vendor flag (USA), bank data ENCRYPTED (account/CLABE/routing — you will never see them in the clear).
- `vendor show --include activity` IS PER CURRENCY (MNE-001-741): `activity.by_currency` gives, per currency, the bills, the open ones, what was billed, the open balance and what was paid, in that currency and at book value. `billed`, `open_balance` and `paid` are book values in `book_currency`, the entity's functional currency (each document, or payment, at the rate it was posted at), never dollars added to pesos. Open means what `ap reconcile` weighs (approved, posted, partially paid): a draft never reached the ledger and is not owed, so the vendors' open balances add up to the subledger.
- A VENDOR'S TAX ID IS CORRECTED WITH `vendor tax set` (MNE-001-664), by a person: `vendor tax set <vendor> --tax-id <id> --reason "<evidence>"` (`--tax-id-type rfc|ein|vat`, by default the type the vendor has, then the entity's country). It is the remedy when the DIOT reports `DIOT-SIN-RFC` or the vouchers file says the RFC is missing or malformed on the vendor's record; `vendor edit` and the REST update do not reach the tax id. It refuses a malformed id, a missing reason and an id another vendor of the entity already carries, and writes nothing then. The audit row keeps the old id, the new one, who and why. No bill, payment or entry is rewritten: tell the user to generate the DIOT or the vouchers again afterwards, and never run it yourself.
- YOU: search_vendors. Human: POST/PATCH /v1/vendors, `vendor create|edit`, `vendor terms set`, `vendor tax set`.

## Vendor invoices (bills)
- States: draft → pending_approval → approved → posted → partially_paid → paid; also void, cancelled.
- Automatic entry when a bill posts: debit expense per line; debit input VAT; credit AP for the total.
- Each line of a manually created bill stores what the DIOT reads (#284): the VAT rate its amounts declare (16 % or 8 % when the tax matches within one cent, 0 % when there is no tax, otherwise the exact ratio, which the DIOT reports in its own "other rates" box), factor type `tasa`, and its line amount as the value of the acts. A manual line cannot be declared exempt; an exempt purchase enters through its CFDI, which carries `TipoFactor="Exento"` and its base.
- Vendor payment: credit BANKS for the cash that left. The debit splits by what the payment actually settles: AP for the amount APPLIED to bills, and `anticipo_proveedores` (1150) for whatever was paid without naming a bill — money out with no bill named is an advance, not a settled liability, and posting it to AP would move the control account while no subledger row moved (exactly what `ap reconcile` reports).
- An early-payment discount credits `devolucion_compras` (5200, contra-cost) and settles MORE liability than the cash: bill 1160, cash 1100, discount 60 → AP debited 1160. On a bill whose VAT waits in 1135 the discount carries its share of that VAT (MNE-001-597): 60 × 160 / 1160 = 8.28, never creditable, because cash basis credits what was paid (LIVA art. 5 fr. III) and a discount lowers the price and its VAT (art. 7). That share leaves 1135 in the same entry, against the discount and not against the expense, so 5200 takes 51.72 and 1130 the 151.72 the cash paid; the application stores it (`discount_iva_amount`, migration 341). In another currency it leaves at the bill's rate, as a pair beside the discount line. The VAT declared does not change: the DIOT and the IVA paper read what the cash paid. A discount posted before migration 341 left that share in 1135; such a row stores NULL and nothing in this release moves it. `amount_paid` only ever grows by the CASH: a discount is not money the vendor received, and counting it as such breaks bank reconciliation. THE WINDOW IS COUNTED IN CALENDAR DAYS (#243): both ends are reduced to their calendar parts before subtracting, so `2/10 Net 30` on a bill dated the 1st ends on the 11th in every timezone. It used to subtract milliseconds between a string (UTC midnight) and the Date pg builds from a DATE column (LOCAL midnight), so west of Greenwich the count came out one short and a payment on day eleven was granted a discount that had already expired.
- Foreign-currency bill payment (R4): the liability is settled at the DOCUMENT's rate (what it was born worth); the cash leaves at the PAYMENT's rate — that rate resolves from the `fuente_tipo_cambio` policy (DOF by default) and FAILS CLOSED if the chosen source has no rate for the payment date, even if another source does. A foreign-currency bill is born with its payable at total × rate to the cent, always with its amount in the bill's currency; a cent its converted lines may miss it by is one more line where the panel key `fx_conversion_cent_difference` says (MNE-001-862), and a bill paid in parts is settled telescopically, so the last payment takes what the payable still holds and it ends at zero. The realized difference lands in 4320 (Utilidad Cambiaria) or 6320 (Pérdida Cambiaria), pro-rata on partial payments, and includes the PPD VAT gap: 1135 releases at the bill's rate while 1130 takes the VAT actually paid at the payment day's rate (LIVA art. 5-III, art. 20 CFF). Paying from a bank account kept in another currency refuses with FX_BANK_CURRENCY_MISMATCH; the payment log records the difference and the rate applied. If a user asks why a payment stopped for a missing DOF rate, the answer is `fx rate set` with the published value — the engine never borrows the FIX for a DOF firm.
- AN ADVANCE IN ANOTHER CURRENCY IS REFUSED (T23b), and this side was the worse of the two: with no bill to take the currency from, the fallback was a LITERAL — `documentos[0]?.moneda ?? 'MXN'` — so every pure vendor advance was written as pesos without asking the vendor (whose column defaults to USD), the caller, or the entity what it keeps its books in. The refusal names both currencies and says the rate and its SOURCE are the firm's decision (`fuente_tipo_cambio`). Tell the user to apply the payment to a bill in its own currency, or to record the advance in the functional one.
- Applying later (`payment apply`): an advance can be spread over bills afterwards — DR AP / CR 1150, and for any bill whose VAT is parked in 1135, whatever its payment method, the parked VAT is released pro-rata. NO cash moves: it already left with the payment. `payment apply` REFUSES a bill of another vendor than the payment's (#664): the refusal names the bill and both vendors, and nothing is written; tell the user to record the payment under the bill's vendor.
- THE APPLICATION IS DATED (MNE-001-678, #787): `payment apply --date <YYYY-MM-DD>`, or today in the entity's time zone (`zona_horaria`) when no date is given. That day goes on the entry, so the VAT the application credits lands in that day's month in the ledger; on the application row (`applied_on`); and on the bill's last payment date, which is never moved back. It REFUSES, naming the date and writing nothing, a date after today, a date before the payment's last posted entry (the payment itself, an earlier application or unapply, a cheque's cashing), a date before the day the bank cashed the payment's cheque (MNE-001-746: a cheque cashed while still on account posts no entry, and its application would credit the VAT before the cheque was paid; the message names the cashing day, the first day it takes), a date before the bill's own date, and a date with no open fiscal period (hard-closed, locked or never opened). An early-payment discount is still judged on the PAYMENT's date, not the application's: the vendor grants it for when the cash left. `payment create` stores the payment's own date on each application it records; a row written before this stores none. THE DIOT AND THE IVA WORKPAPER COUNT THE APPLICATION ON THAT SAME DAY (MNE-001-679): cash paid on account on March 20th and applied on May 10th is declared in May, 1,000.00 / 160.00 on a bill of 1,160.00, traced to the application's entry, and March and April declare nothing for it; two applications in two months count each in its own. A cheque counts on the later of that day and its cashing. A row that stores no day (written before MNE-001-678) is still counted on its PAYMENT's date, so a month already shown does not move: if its entry was posted in a later month, the workpaper's tie-out `IVA-WP-CREDITABLE-VS-LEDGER` names the difference in both months. Say it; do not present such a month as tied out.
- Short pay (`payment apply --mode residual`, requires a written reason): the bill closes for less than it owed and the shortfall stops being owed. WHERE it goes is the firm's policy (`pago_corto_residual` in the panel: contra-cost, other income, or forbidden) — never assume it, and if the firm has not decided, say the default is being used. On a bill whose VAT waits in 1135 (a PPD, or a PUE since MNE-001-492) the VAT on the unpaid part leaves 1135 WITHOUT becoming creditable: cash basis credits what was paid, and that part never will be.
- VAT released above a CFDI's VAT (MNE-001-562): until MNE-001-488 a vendor CFDI with IEPS kept VAT plus IEPS in its bill's header, so its payments moved part of the IEPS from 1135 to 1130 as VAT. `npm run correct:vat-overcredit -- --tenant <uuid>` lists each such bill, its payments, the excess per month (the figure of each complementary return, CFF art. 32) and why a bill is not corrected automatically (closed short, a credit note, another currency, a header edited by hand, an open bill with the old header, a release never stored). It writes nothing unless a PERSON adds `--apply`: never run it yourself. Then each bill gets an audited entry DR 1135 / CR 1130, source `vat_overcredit_correction` and the bill as source, dated as the panel key `vat_overcredit_correction_period` says (`ask` by default: nothing posts; `current_period`; `payment_month`, where a closed month needs `--reopen` and a locked one is never reopened). If a user asks why such an entry exists, that is the answer; if a corrected payment is later unapplied, the census reports the bill over-corrected and the remedy is reversing the correction and running it again.
- The VAT of a discount posted before migration 341 (MNE-001-599): such a discount went whole to 5200 and its share of the bill's VAT stayed in 1135 (8.00 on 1,160 paid with 1,102 + 58), and its application row stores `discount_iva_amount` NULL. `npm run correct:discount-vat -- --tenant <uuid>` lists each bill that still has VAT parked, with the share of each discount per payment and per month (the month of the entry that posted the discount), capped by what the bill still has in 1135, and names the bills it does not correct automatically (another currency, closed short, a header that disagrees with what the bill parked). It writes nothing unless a PERSON adds `--apply`: never run it yourself. Then each bill and payment gets an audited entry DR 5200 / CR 1135, source `discount_vat_correction` and the PAYMENT as source, and the row stores the share in place of its NULL; it is dated as the panel key `discount_vat_correction_period` says (`ask` by default: nothing posts; `current_period`; `payment_month`, where a closed month needs `--reopen` and a locked one is never reopened). The VAT declared does not change; the ISR deductions of the month the entry is dated in rise by the share. A later `payment unapply` gives back exactly that share, and `payment reverse` mirrors the correction with the payment's other entries; do not reverse a correction entry by hand, because the row would still say its VAT left.
- Undoing an application (`payment unapply --bill --reason [--date]`, T11): a NEW dated event, never a delete. The application row is closed (`unapplied_at`, who, why), the bill is owed again (back to approved or partially_paid) and the entry, dated `--date`, is DR 1150 (the cash is back on account — unapplying is not a refund) plus DR 5200 for any discount the application took / CR AP, with the VAT it released re-parked 1130 → 1135 for the exact amount its row stored. The discount's VAT that left 1135 (MNE-001-597) goes back to it too, so 5200 takes back only the cost part; a row written before migration 341 gives back the discount whole, because its VAT never left. The date cannot precede the payment's last entry, and with no `--date` it is today in the entity's time zone (MNE-001-678). It REFUSES, rather than guess, a bill closed short (the written-off part is stored nowhere), a foreign-currency payment, and an application written before migration 105 in a cash-basis entity (it never stored its VAT).
- Reversing a payment that came back (`payment reverse <payment> --reason [--date]`, T11): not the same as unapplying. Unapplying keeps the payment and puts its cash back on account; reversing undoes the PAYMENT — a duplicated transfer the vendor or the bank sent back. Every entry the payment posted (the payment, each later application and unapplication, and the cashing entry of a cheque) gets its NIF B-1 mirror dated `--date`, so VAT and exchange legs come back exactly as they went; the bills it still had applied are owed again (amount plus any discount), those applications are closed on that date, and the payment turns `reversed` with `reversed_at` (migration 118: it happened and was undone, which is not `void`). The date cannot precede the payment's last entry. It REFUSES a bill closed short, as unapply does. A reader that only excludes `void` (the missing-REP check) still counts a reversed payment: flag it to the human rather than assuming either way.
- BY DEFAULT THE DIOT AND THE IVA WORKPAPER TAKE AN UNDONE APPLICATION OUT IN THE MONTH OF THE UNDO, NEVER IN THE MONTH IT COUNTED IN (MNE-001-682, #855): they follow the ledger, which credits 1130 on the day of the `payment unapply` or the `payment reverse` (the day the application row stores, `unapplied_at`). A bill of 1,000.00 + 160.00 paid in April and unapplied or reversed on May 12th is declared 1,000.00 / 160.00 in April, whenever April is built, and −1,000.00 / −160.00 in May, traced to the unapplication's entry or the reversal's mirrors; the VAT withheld to the vendor follows the same months; an undo dated in the application's own month nets to 0.00 there, and a cheque nobody cashed never counted, so its undo takes nothing out. Unlike a receipt, whose VAT stays caused after an unapply, the vendor side gives the credit up: creditable VAT needs the tax paid on a bill (LIVA art. 5 fr. III), and the bill is owed again. May's DIOT carries the notice `DIOT-PAYMENT-UNDONE`: if the application should never have existed (the money never left, or it paid another bill), April's return may need an amended one instead (CFF art. 32); say it, and do not decide it for the accountant. In the DIOT the negative document nets inside its third party's row; the SAT batch file carries no negative amount, so `diot export --layout sat` refuses, by name, a third party whose month nets negative, and that one is captured in the portal. That is the answer `undo_month` of the panel key `vendor_undo_iva_month` (MNE-001-754). With `payment_month` the firm amends instead: the undone application counts in no month, so April built after the undo no longer shows the bill and its return needs an amended one, and May takes nothing out; both months carry `DIOT-PAYMENT-UNDONE`, naming the answer, and the workpaper's tie-out `IVA-WP-CREDITABLE-VS-LEDGER` names the bill in both, because the ledger still moved 1130 in each. No month nets negative under that answer. Say which answer the firm gave; never choose it for them.
- A cheque to a vendor (MNE-001-508, #700): a price paid by cheque is paid the day the bank cashes it. While a payment with `--method check` in the entity's functional currency has no cashing recorded, neither its own entry nor a later `payment apply` credits VAT: the VAT stays in 1135 and the application stores 0 released. `bank check reconcile` records the cashing against the bank charge and posts DR 1130 / CR 1135 dated that charge's day, for what the payment has applied and not yet released, or on the day the application was dated when that is later than the charge (MNE-001-778: applied today and reconciled against last month's statement, a cheque the bank charged on March 10th and applied with `--date 2026-05-10` releases its 160.00 on May 10th, the month the DIOT and the workpaper count it; the cashing keeps the bank's day, and `--json` gives the entry's day as `entry_date`). When the applications it would release fall on more than one such day, it REFUSES, naming the days and writing nothing, because one cashing posts one entry with one date: unapply the later ones, record the cashing, and apply them again. An application made after the cashing releases as any other. A cheque whose own entry already credited the VAT (every cheque recorded before this rule, and a foreign-currency cheque still) records its cashing and moves nothing. The IVA workpaper and the DIOT follow the ledger (MNE-001-559): a cheque that held its VAT is counted in the month of its cashing, and with no cashing recorded in no month, where each month from the signing one lists the notice `DIOT-CHEQUE-NOT-CASHED` with the cheque, its date and the VAT it holds, without blocking the settlement. A cheque whose own entry credited the VAT keeps its payment month; if the bank cashed it in a later month, that month lists `DIOT-CHEQUE-CREDITED-BEFORE-CASHING`, naming the cashing month and that a return already filed may need an amended one. An advance by cheque applied to a bill counts on the later of its cashing and the day it was applied (MNE-001-679): applied before the cashing, in the cashing month; applied after it, in the month of the application, where the ledger credits it. An application whose row stores no day (written before MNE-001-678) is counted in the cashing month, and the workpaper's tie-out `IVA-WP-CREDITABLE-VS-LEDGER` says so when its entry is in another.
- Cash above the LISR art. 27 fr. III limit (MNE-001-345): `payment create --method cash` (a pure cash advance included) and `bill approve` (for a bill whose CFDI says FormaPago 01, and that the CFDI ingestion did not create: its `efectivo_no_deducible` answer stands) return a non-blocking `deductibilityFindings` list (CLI: stderr before the confirmation and after, and `deductibility_findings` in `--json`; REST: `data.deductibility_findings` on the payment, `meta.deductibility_findings` on approve) when the amount exceeds `income_tax.cash_payment_deduction_limit` IN FORCE ON THE PAYMENT DATE (the bill date at approval, an approximation: the CFDI declares cash, the payment may later be by transfer or in instalments). The entry is NOT changed. What else happens is the panel key `cash_over_limit_outside_ingestion`: `signal` (default), `draft_reclassification` (a draft moving the bill's expense and IVA to 6900 for a person to approve, one open draft per bill, none in a dry run) or `ignore`; say when the default is the one in use. Only pesos and Mexican-law entities are judged. A date with no vigencia is not silent and does not block: it comes back as a `cash_limit_unavailable` finding.
- A CFDI the inbox holds on a decision about that one document (MNE-001-906): `bill inbox run` refuses it with «Requires a decision: <ids>» and names, per decision, the values it offers. A PERSON answers for that CFDI alone with `mnemosine bill inbox answer <id> <decision> <value>` (REST: `POST /v1/pre-registrations/:id/answer` with `decision` and `answer`; IA ✗: you cannot invoke it, and you never answer for the person), and the next `bill inbox run <id>` uses the answer. It is kept in the classification trail with who gave it and when: `cfdi explain <uuid>` shows the options and the answer. The decisions answered this way are the blocking ones whose every option names the role it posts to: `gasto_vs_activo`, `efectivo_no_deducible`, `combustible_efectivo`, `anticipo_o_gasto`, `por_cuenta_terceros`. A closed period, a cancelled CFDI and an unknown vendor are NOT answered there (panel key `cfdi_periodo_cerrado`, `--action reject`, `--allow-new-vendor`). An option the decision did not offer is refused naming the valid ones. The inbox never posts an entry that ignores an answer, so three answers are kept while the CFDI STAYS HELD, and the refusal says which: one that makes the expense non-deductible (`no_deducible`), because the entry that takes the expense and its IVA to the non-deductible account is not built yet; one the proposed entry has no line for (an advance answered `gasto`); and one whose amount would not land on the account of its role, which is `gasto_vs_activo = activo_fijo` or `inventario` while a line is coded to another account. That last one is released by coding every line to the role's account, which the refusal names (`bill inbox edit <id> --line <n> --account <code>`), and running again.
- A bill can also be born WITH THE OPENING BALANCE (MNE-001-023): `opening-balance import --subledger` turns each open document of the payable control account into a bill in status approved, linked to the opening entry (it posts nothing of its own: the opening line already carries the liability), with one line carrying the document's IVA rate (`ivaRate`): base as subtotal, the IVA as tax still pending, released to creditable when it is paid, and declared by rate in the DIOT of that month. A document without the rate follows the panel key `opening_payable_iva` (default `require_rate`: the load stops). The vendor's folio goes in `vendor_invoice_number` and the next BILL folio is its own number; a generic RFC (XAXX/XEXX) is matched by name, never by RFC. Only the account the `cxp` role points at becomes bills; documents of another payable account stay opening lines, with a warning. The load stops if a document is a debit balance or in another currency, if the same vendor folio or CFDI UUID is already a bill, if the IVA of the documents is not in the `iva_pendiente_acreditar` account of the opening, or if the documents do not add up to the account's balance (it says what is missing or left over, per account, and posts no adjustment). A reload after voiding the opening voids the untouched bills of the reversed one that the new file no longer brings. It is paid like any other, and `ap reconcile` does not list the opening as a manual entry once bills hang from it.
- VOIDING AN APPROVED BILL (MNE-001-947, #1079; part 1 of 2): `voidBill` (bill-service.ts) annuls a bill that is approved, or posted from the inbox, and owed whole, in one transaction: the mirror of its entry (NIF B-1: a correction by reversal), the bill at `void` with 0.00 due, and an audit row with the reason. On a bill of 10,000.00 + 1,600.00 the mirror is DR 2110 11,600.00 / CR the expense 10,000.00 / CR 1135 1,600.00: the VAT parked in 1135 leaves with it, PUE or PPD, and so does any IEPS or withholding line the bill's own entry carries, in the mirror's month. It is refused, and nothing is written, with a live payment application (undo it first with `payment unapply`, which puts the VAT that payment released back in 1135), with another entry standing on the bill (a received credit note, a VAT over-credit correction of MNE-001-562), for a bill born from the opening balance (its entry is the whole opening's) and for a bill of another entity, which answers as a missing one. An entry somebody already reversed by hand gets no second mirror: the bill is closed against the one that exists, and `ap reconcile` ties again. THE MIRROR'S DATE is the panel key `bill_void_mirror_date`: `void_date` (default) is the day of the void in the entity's time zone, as `invoice void` dates its own, so no closed or declared month moves; `bill_date` is the day of the bill's entry, and when no open period holds that day `cfdi_periodo_cerrado` decides: only `periodo_actual` dates it the day of the void, any other answer refuses. It cancels nothing before the SAT, and it corrects neither a month already declared nor a withholding already remitted: that is the accountant's call. NO COMMAND CALLS IT YET: `bill void` arrives with part 2. Until then tell a human that an approved bill cannot be voided from the terminal, and never to reverse its entry by hand: `entry reverse` takes the ledger back, leaves the bill open and the close stops on `ap-subledger-delta`.
- UNTIL WHEN A VENDOR MAY CANCEL A CFDI (CFF art. 29-A; source: Cámara de Diputados, «Código Fiscal de la Federación», text with its last reform DOF 09-04-2026, https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/CFF.pdf, read 2026-11-27; the paragraph was reformed DOF 12-11-2021 and 07-11-2025): «Los comprobantes fiscales digitales por Internet se podrán cancelar a más tardar en el mes en el cual se deba presentar la declaración anual del impuesto sobre la renta que corresponda al ejercicio fiscal en el cual se expidió el referido comprobante y siempre que la persona a favor de quien se expidan acepte su cancelación.» So a cancellation can reach a bill whose month is already closed and declared. The bill keeps the mark (`cfdi_cancellation_noted_at`) and `ap check` lists it; what the cancellation means for the deduction and the VAT already declared is the accountant's call.
- The bulk entry path for bills in Mexico is CFDI ingestion (mexico-cfdi doc): pre-registration → rules → your classification.

## Human (REST /v1/bills)
- POST / (create), POST /:id/approve, POST /payments (payment applied to bills — this is the call that moves amount_due and posts to the ledger).
- Approving is scoped to the ACTIVE entity, on every surface. A bill that belongs to a sibling company of the same firm answers 404 — the very same 404 as a bill id that does not exist, on purpose, so the answer never confirms that another company's bill is there. Until TEN-11 (#235) the REST route did not scope at all: approving the sibling's bill posted an entry into ITS ledger and consumed its folio. If a human says a bill "is not found" but they can see it, the first question is which entity their session is in.
- CLI: `bill create|approve`, `payment create` (pay a bill), `payment apply` (spread a payment already made), `payment unapply` (undo one application), `payment reverse` (undo the whole payment), `bill inbox list|run` (the CFDI queue). Payment writes are IA ✗ — you may read and propose, never run them.
- POST /:id/schedule-payment answers 501: mnemosine has no payment scheduler and no connection to any bank. It used to answer 200 and append a line to bills.memo, which left the vendor unpaid on the date it reported. Never tell a human a payment is scheduled.
- Payment trace for the Anexo 24 (F07d): when a payment moves money, the póliza XML must carry the trail inside it — origin account and bank, destination account and bank, cheque number, date, payee and their RFC. `payment create` now captures the cheque number and the destination account (nationally by SAT bank code, or by NAME for a foreign bank: they are different fields, and a CHECK refuses both at once). The cheque number was a column three call sites READ and no INSERT ever wrote — orphan capacity in reverse. The destination lives on the PAYMENT, not on the vendor: the filing declares the account of THAT movement, so reading it from the master would make an old póliza change its answer when the vendor's details change. A payment that moved money without its trail is reported by póliza number rather than silently omitted. AT CAPTURE (MNE-001-571), once `payment create` or `payment trace record` writes, stderr repeats the finding `voucher generate` will make of that voucher, word for word, with the command that completes it (a missing bank account and a bank account with no SAT bank code included); nothing under `--json`. Relay it to the user while the receipt or stub is at hand. A PAYMENT ALREADY REGISTERED IS COMPLETED, NEVER CAPTURED AGAIN (MNE-001-568, #612): `payment trace record <payment> --to-account <number> --to-bank <c_Banco> --reason "<evidence>"` (or `--check-number`, `--to-foreign-bank`) fills only EMPTY fields of a `completed` or `reversed` payment from its evidence (the SPEI receipt, the cheque stub, the statement). The same value again is reported unchanged; a different one is refused, naming the stored one (an account by its last four digits): correcting a captured value is not this command. The capture's rules apply to the merged trace (no cheque number on a transfer, a bank needs an account, never a national and a foreign bank together). It posts nothing, so it runs in any period state, a hard-closed one included, and says the state when the month is not open; each completion leaves one `audit_log` row (`complete_trace`) with the reason, the period state, the entry and how many voucher files of that month were already archived, which nothing rewrites. The voucher finding names this command with the payment's number. Never tell the user to reverse and pay again: that leaves the original entry and its mirror untraced. A payment registered without `--bank` gets its bank account linked the same way, `payment trace record <payment> --bank "<account>" --reason "<evidence>"` (MNE-001-569), accepted only when the payment has a posted entry, the ledger account that bank account posts to is on a line of that entry, and its currency is the payment's; any other account is refused, naming both ledger accounts, because the voucher would declare a bank the ledger did not move. An inactive account is accepted (this is history). `--to-bank` is checked against the SAT c_Banco list, here and in `payment create`: an off-list code is refused before anything is written. A foreign destination bank, given by its name (`--to-foreign-bank`), goes to the voucher as `BancoDestNal="999"` with the name in `BancoDestExt` (MNE-001-401); `999` together with a name is refused (give the name alone), and a bare `--to-bank 999` is accepted with a warning at capture and a `banco-en-catalogo` warning on its voucher, because `999` is «N/A» in c_Banco and names no bank.
A bill with a received credit note can have its payments unapplied or reversed (MNE-001-582): the note's payable debit counts as explained, so only a real short pay (`--mode residual`) is refused as closed short; the IVA that goes back to 1135 is the stored release, which is already the IVA of the balance owed again, because a received note never exceeds what the bill owes and takes its IVA from 1135 first.
