# Accounts payable (vendors and received invoices)

## Vendors
- Creating a vendor leaves an audit row (G3): who, when, and which fields — bank details never travel into `audit_log`, not even already-encrypted, because that table is append-only (033) and a CLABE that lands there does not come out even by rotating the key; what is recorded is `bank_details_on_file: true`.
- Data: vendor_number (V-...), company_name, tax_id (RFC/EIN), terms, default expense account, is_1099_vendor flag (USA), bank data ENCRYPTED (account/CLABE/routing — you will never see them in the clear).
- YOU: search_vendors. Human: POST/PATCH /v1/vendors.

## Vendor invoices (bills)
- States: draft → pending_approval → approved → posted → partially_paid → paid; also void, cancelled.
- Automatic entry when a bill posts: debit expense per line; debit input VAT; credit AP for the total.
- Each line of a manually created bill stores what the DIOT reads (#284): the VAT rate its amounts declare (16 % or 8 % when the tax matches within one cent, 0 % when there is no tax, otherwise the exact ratio, which the DIOT reports in its own "other rates" box), factor type `tasa`, and its line amount as the value of the acts. A manual line cannot be declared exempt; an exempt purchase enters through its CFDI, which carries `TipoFactor="Exento"` and its base.
- Vendor payment: credit BANKS for the cash that left. The debit splits by what the payment actually settles: AP for the amount APPLIED to bills, and `anticipo_proveedores` (1150) for whatever was paid without naming a bill — money out with no bill named is an advance, not a settled liability, and posting it to AP would move the control account while no subledger row moved (exactly what `ap reconcile` reports).
- An early-payment discount credits `devolucion_compras` (5200, contra-cost) and settles MORE liability than the cash: bill 1160, cash 1100, discount 60 → AP debited 1160. `amount_paid` only ever grows by the CASH: a discount is not money the vendor received, and counting it as such breaks bank reconciliation. THE WINDOW IS COUNTED IN CALENDAR DAYS (#243): both ends are reduced to their calendar parts before subtracting, so `2/10 Net 30` on a bill dated the 1st ends on the 11th in every timezone. It used to subtract milliseconds between a string (UTC midnight) and the Date pg builds from a DATE column (LOCAL midnight), so west of Greenwich the count came out one short and a payment on day eleven was granted a discount that had already expired.
- Foreign-currency bill payment (R4): the liability is settled at the DOCUMENT's rate (what it was born worth); the cash leaves at the PAYMENT's rate — that rate resolves from the `fuente_tipo_cambio` policy (DOF by default) and FAILS CLOSED if the chosen source has no rate for the payment date, even if another source does. The realized difference lands in 4320 (Utilidad Cambiaria) or 6320 (Pérdida Cambiaria), pro-rata on partial payments, and includes the PPD VAT gap: 1135 releases at the bill's rate while 1130 takes the VAT actually paid at the payment day's rate (LIVA art. 5-III, art. 20 CFF). Paying from a bank account kept in another currency refuses with FX_BANK_CURRENCY_MISMATCH; the payment log records the difference and the rate applied. If a user asks why a payment stopped for a missing DOF rate, the answer is `fx rate set` with the published value — the engine never borrows the FIX for a DOF firm.
- AN ADVANCE IN ANOTHER CURRENCY IS REFUSED (T23b), and this side was the worse of the two: with no bill to take the currency from, the fallback was a LITERAL — `documentos[0]?.moneda ?? 'MXN'` — so every pure vendor advance was written as pesos without asking the vendor (whose column defaults to USD), the caller, or the entity what it keeps its books in. The refusal names both currencies and says the rate and its SOURCE are the firm's decision (`fuente_tipo_cambio`). Tell the user to apply the payment to a bill in its own currency, or to record the advance in the functional one.
- Applying later (`payment apply`): an advance can be spread over bills afterwards — DR AP / CR 1150, and for any bill whose VAT is parked in 1135, whatever its payment method, the parked VAT is released pro-rata. NO cash moves: it already left with the payment. `payment apply` REFUSES a bill of another vendor than the payment's (#664): the refusal names the bill and both vendors, and nothing is written; tell the user to record the payment under the bill's vendor.
- Short pay (`payment apply --mode residual`, requires a written reason): the bill closes for less than it owed and the shortfall stops being owed. WHERE it goes is the firm's policy (`pago_corto_residual` in the panel: contra-cost, other income, or forbidden) — never assume it, and if the firm has not decided, say the default is being used. On a bill whose VAT waits in 1135 (a PPD, or a PUE since MNE-001-492) the VAT on the unpaid part leaves 1135 WITHOUT becoming creditable: cash basis credits what was paid, and that part never will be.
- VAT released above a CFDI's VAT (MNE-001-562): until MNE-001-488 a vendor CFDI with IEPS kept VAT plus IEPS in its bill's header, so its payments moved part of the IEPS from 1135 to 1130 as VAT. `npm run correct:vat-overcredit -- --tenant <uuid>` lists each such bill, its payments, the excess per month (the figure of each complementary return, CFF art. 32) and why a bill is not corrected automatically (closed short, a credit note, another currency, a header edited by hand, an open bill with the old header, a release never stored). It writes nothing unless a PERSON adds `--apply`: never run it yourself. Then each bill gets an audited entry DR 1135 / CR 1130, source `vat_overcredit_correction` and the bill as source, dated as the panel key `vat_overcredit_correction_period` says (`ask` by default: nothing posts; `current_period`; `payment_month`, where a closed month needs `--reopen` and a locked one is never reopened). If a user asks why such an entry exists, that is the answer; if a corrected payment is later unapplied, the census reports the bill over-corrected and the remedy is reversing the correction and running it again.
- Undoing an application (`payment unapply --bill --reason [--date]`, T11): a NEW dated event, never a delete. The application row is closed (`unapplied_at`, who, why), the bill is owed again (back to approved or partially_paid) and the entry, dated `--date`, is DR 1150 (the cash is back on account — unapplying is not a refund) plus DR 5200 for any discount the application took / CR AP, with the VAT it released re-parked 1130 → 1135 for the exact amount its row stored. The date cannot precede the payment's last entry. It REFUSES, rather than guess, a bill closed short (the written-off part is stored nowhere), a foreign-currency payment, and an application written before migration 105 in a cash-basis entity (it never stored its VAT).
- Reversing a payment that came back (`payment reverse <payment> --reason [--date]`, T11): not the same as unapplying. Unapplying keeps the payment and puts its cash back on account; reversing undoes the PAYMENT — a duplicated transfer the vendor or the bank sent back. Every entry the payment posted (the payment, each later application and unapplication, and the cashing entry of a cheque) gets its NIF B-1 mirror dated `--date`, so VAT and exchange legs come back exactly as they went; the bills it still had applied are owed again (amount plus any discount), those applications are closed on that date, and the payment turns `reversed` with `reversed_at` (migration 118: it happened and was undone, which is not `void`). The date cannot precede the payment's last entry. It REFUSES a bill closed short, as unapply does. Readers that only exclude `void` (the DIOT facts, the missing-REP check) still count a reversed payment: flag it to the human rather than assuming either way.
- A cheque to a vendor (MNE-001-508, #700): a price paid by cheque is paid the day the bank cashes it. While a payment with `--method check` in the entity's functional currency has no cashing recorded, neither its own entry nor a later `payment apply` credits VAT: the VAT stays in 1135 and the application stores 0 released. `bank check reconcile` records the cashing against the bank charge and posts DR 1130 / CR 1135 dated that charge's day, for what the payment has applied and not yet released; an application made after the cashing releases as any other. A cheque whose own entry already credited the VAT (every cheque recorded before this rule, and a foreign-currency cheque still) records its cashing and moves nothing. The IVA workpaper and the DIOT follow the ledger (MNE-001-559): a cheque that held its VAT is counted in the month of its cashing, and with no cashing recorded in no month, where each month from the signing one lists the notice `DIOT-CHEQUE-NOT-CASHED` with the cheque, its date and the VAT it holds, without blocking the settlement. A cheque whose own entry credited the VAT keeps its payment month; if the bank cashed it in a later month, that month lists `DIOT-CHEQUE-CREDITED-BEFORE-CASHING`, naming the cashing month and that a return already filed may need an amended one. The rule is per payment: an advance by cheque applied after its cashing is counted in the cashing month, while the ledger credits it on the day of the application, and the workpaper's tie-out `IVA-WP-CREDITABLE-VS-LEDGER` says so.
- Cash above the LISR art. 27 fr. III limit (MNE-001-345): `payment create --method cash` (a pure cash advance included) and `bill approve` (for a bill whose CFDI says FormaPago 01, and that the CFDI ingestion did not create: its `efectivo_no_deducible` answer stands) return a non-blocking `deductibilityFindings` list (CLI: stderr before the confirmation and after, and `deductibility_findings` in `--json`; REST: `data.deductibility_findings` on the payment, `meta.deductibility_findings` on approve) when the amount exceeds `income_tax.cash_payment_deduction_limit` IN FORCE ON THE PAYMENT DATE (the bill date at approval, an approximation: the CFDI declares cash, the payment may later be by transfer or in instalments). The entry is NOT changed. What else happens is the panel key `cash_over_limit_outside_ingestion`: `signal` (default), `draft_reclassification` (a draft moving the bill's expense and IVA to 6900 for a person to approve, one open draft per bill, none in a dry run) or `ignore`; say when the default is the one in use. Only pesos and Mexican-law entities are judged. A date with no vigencia is not silent and does not block: it comes back as a `cash_limit_unavailable` finding.
- A bill can also be born WITH THE OPENING BALANCE (MNE-001-023): `opening-balance import --subledger` turns each open document of the payable control account into a bill in status approved, linked to the opening entry (it posts nothing of its own: the opening line already carries the liability), with one line carrying the document's IVA rate (`ivaRate`): base as subtotal, the IVA as tax still pending, released to creditable when it is paid, and declared by rate in the DIOT of that month. A document without the rate follows the panel key `opening_payable_iva` (default `require_rate`: the load stops). The vendor's folio goes in `vendor_invoice_number` and the next BILL folio is its own number; a generic RFC (XAXX/XEXX) is matched by name, never by RFC. Only the account the `cxp` role points at becomes bills; documents of another payable account stay opening lines, with a warning. The load stops if a document is a debit balance or in another currency, if the same vendor folio or CFDI UUID is already a bill, if the IVA of the documents is not in the `iva_pendiente_acreditar` account of the opening, or if the documents do not add up to the account's balance (it says what is missing or left over, per account, and posts no adjustment). A reload after voiding the opening voids the untouched bills of the reversed one that the new file no longer brings. It is paid like any other, and `ap reconcile` does not list the opening as a manual entry once bills hang from it.
- The bulk entry path for bills in Mexico is CFDI ingestion (mexico-cfdi doc): pre-registration → rules → your classification.

## Human (REST /v1/bills)
- POST / (create), POST /:id/approve, POST /payments (payment applied to bills — this is the call that moves amount_due and posts to the ledger).
- Approving is scoped to the ACTIVE entity, on every surface. A bill that belongs to a sibling company of the same firm answers 404 — the very same 404 as a bill id that does not exist, on purpose, so the answer never confirms that another company's bill is there. Until TEN-11 (#235) the REST route did not scope at all: approving the sibling's bill posted an entry into ITS ledger and consumed its folio. If a human says a bill "is not found" but they can see it, the first question is which entity their session is in.
- CLI: `bill create|approve`, `payment create` (pay a bill), `payment apply` (spread a payment already made), `payment unapply` (undo one application), `payment reverse` (undo the whole payment), `bill inbox list|run` (the CFDI queue). Payment writes are IA ✗ — you may read and propose, never run them.
- POST /:id/schedule-payment answers 501: mnemosine has no payment scheduler and no connection to any bank. It used to answer 200 and append a line to bills.memo, which left the vendor unpaid on the date it reported. Never tell a human a payment is scheduled.
- Payment trace for the Anexo 24 (F07d): when a payment moves money, the póliza XML must carry the trail inside it — origin account and bank, destination account and bank, cheque number, date, payee and their RFC. `payment create` now captures the cheque number and the destination account (nationally by SAT bank code, or by NAME for a foreign bank: they are different fields, and a CHECK refuses both at once). The cheque number was a column three call sites READ and no INSERT ever wrote — orphan capacity in reverse. The destination lives on the PAYMENT, not on the vendor: the filing declares the account of THAT movement, so reading it from the master would make an old póliza change its answer when the vendor's details change. A payment that moved money without its trail is reported by póliza number rather than silently omitted. AT CAPTURE (MNE-001-571), once `payment create` or `payment trace record` writes, stderr repeats the finding `voucher generate` will make of that voucher, word for word, with the command that completes it (a missing bank account and a bank account with no SAT bank code included); nothing under `--json`. Relay it to the user while the receipt or stub is at hand. A PAYMENT ALREADY REGISTERED IS COMPLETED, NEVER CAPTURED AGAIN (MNE-001-568, #612): `payment trace record <payment> --to-account <number> --to-bank <c_Banco> --reason "<evidence>"` (or `--check-number`, `--to-foreign-bank`) fills only EMPTY fields of a `completed` or `reversed` payment from its evidence (the SPEI receipt, the cheque stub, the statement). The same value again is reported unchanged; a different one is refused, naming the stored one (an account by its last four digits): correcting a captured value is not this command. The capture's rules apply to the merged trace (no cheque number on a transfer, a bank needs an account, never a national and a foreign bank together). It posts nothing, so it runs in any period state, a hard-closed one included, and says the state when the month is not open; each completion leaves one `audit_log` row (`complete_trace`) with the reason, the period state, the entry and how many voucher files of that month were already archived, which nothing rewrites. The voucher finding names this command with the payment's number. Never tell the user to reverse and pay again: that leaves the original entry and its mirror untraced. A payment registered without `--bank` gets its bank account linked the same way, `payment trace record <payment> --bank "<account>" --reason "<evidence>"` (MNE-001-569), accepted only when the payment has a posted entry, the ledger account that bank account posts to is on a line of that entry, and its currency is the payment's; any other account is refused, naming both ledger accounts, because the voucher would declare a bank the ledger did not move. An inactive account is accepted (this is history). `--to-bank` is checked against the SAT c_Banco list, here and in `payment create`: an off-list code is refused before anything is written. A foreign destination bank, given by its name (`--to-foreign-bank`), goes to the voucher as `BancoDestNal="999"` with the name in `BancoDestExt` (MNE-001-401); `999` together with a name is refused (give the name alone), and a bare `--to-bank 999` is accepted with a warning at capture and a `banco-en-catalogo` warning on its voucher, because `999` is «N/A» in c_Banco and names no bank.
A bill with a received credit note can have its payments unapplied or reversed (MNE-001-582): the note's payable debit counts as explained, so only a real short pay (`--mode residual`) is refused as closed short; the IVA that goes back to 1135 is the stored release, which is already the IVA of the balance owed again, because a received note never exceeds what the bill owes and takes its IVA from 1135 first.
